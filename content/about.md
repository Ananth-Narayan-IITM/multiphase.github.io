I am a researcher in **computational fluid physics** with a focus on the thermal
management of semiconductor devices. My work sits at the intersection of continuum
mechanics, electromagnetism and numerical optimisation: I build solvers, validate
them against experiment, and use them to design cooling solutions that are smaller,
quieter and more effective.

I completed my PhD on **multiphase and electrohydrodynamic heat transfer**, where I
developed interface-resolved methods for confined Taylor-bubble flows and coupled
charge-transport models for electric-field-enhanced convection. Along the way I grew
fond of *adjoint-based topology optimisation*, because it lets the physics decide the
geometry instead of the other way around.

> *"The equations are the easy part — the hard part is convincing them to agree
> with the experiment."*

## What drives me

I like problems where the physics is genuinely coupled: a bubble changes the flow, the
flow changes the temperature field, and the temperature field changes the material
properties that feed back into the flow. Semiconductor cooling is full of these loops,
and it is exactly where careful numerical work pays off.

## How I work

- **Start from the physics.** I derive or select the model before touching a mesh.
- **Verify, then validate.** Method of manufactured solutions and grid-convergence
  studies come before any claim about a new design.
- **Make it reproducible.** Every campaign lives in version control with a run
  manifest, so a figure can always be traced back to the case that produced it.
- **Communicate clearly.** A result is only finished when it can be explained to a
  process engineer in five minutes.

## Beyond the simulations
